Code of conduct

This page is a reading guide for the repository's CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md, not a copy of it. The original is in English and written as a list of clauses; here it is rewritten into a version you can read at a glance. Where the two differ, the original wins.

Scope: the repository, issues, PRs, reviews, discussions, the wiki, releases, and speaking on behalf of this project in public.

Core agreements

These four are the actual requirements extracted from the original:

1. Respect + assuming good faith. Assume the other person is trying to help, even when the result is wrong.

2. Separate "the work is wrong" from "the person is wrong". Give and accept constructive technical feedback. This is a compiler project, so almost every disagreement is about the work itself.

3. Disagreements have to be substantive. Give reasons, give evidence, and say clearly what would change your mind. This is the standard the original states explicitly for technical discussion — "this design is wrong" is always fine, "I want to win this argument" is not.

4. Report problems precisely and specifically. The original's judgment is: a vague report costs the maintainer more time than it saves.

Two more are also written down in the original: take responsibility, apologize to those affected, and learn from the mistake; focus on making the project and its users better rather than on winning an argument.

Unacceptable behavior

The original lists them briefly; here they are listed faithfully:

  • Sexually explicit language or imagery, and any form of sexual attention or advances.
  • Trolling, insulting or derogatory remarks, personal or political attacks.
  • Public or private harassment.
  • Publishing others' private information without explicit permission — for example their address or email.
  • Other conduct deemed inappropriate in a professional setting.

This is not a "community manifesto". It is a minimum agreement: the five items above are all of it, there are no further hidden clauses.

The maintainer is obliged to protect the reporter's privacy and safety.

Enforcement ladder

The original gives four levels, which the maintainer picks from depending on the situation:

  1. Correction — a private written warning that explains the nature of the behaviour and why it was inappropriate; it may also request a public apology.
  2. Warning — a warning that spells out the consequences of continuing, including a period of not interacting with the people involved.
  3. Temporary ban — a temporary ban on any form of interaction or public communication.
  4. Permanent ban — a permanent ban on any public interaction within this project.

The maintainer's response commitments

What is written down: every report will be "reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly" (original wording: reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly), and the reporter's privacy is protected.

What is not yet written down:

  • The handling time limit. The original uses qualitative wording and gives no specific number of days. This differs from the security disclosure page — that one has explicit 7-day / 14-day targets. There is no matching number on the code of conduct side at the moment; we are working on it.
  • Whether the outcome of a report is communicated back to the reporter, and in what form.
  • Whether a second person (an arbiter / a committee) will be set up to handle reports aimed at the maintainer themselves.

The limits of a single maintainer (the original states this plainly)

This project has only one maintainer, so the person who enforces the code of conduct is the same person who is asked to handle the report — if the subject of the report happens to be the maintainer themselves.

The original does not dodge this point, and neither does this website. The way out the original gives is: use GitHub's own abuse reporting channel, or simply stop corresponding.

We put this here because hiding it would only hurt the reporter at the moment it is actually needed.

Relation to the upstream standard

The repository's CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md is adapted from Contributor Covenant version 2.1, and the idea behind the enforcement ladder comes from Mozilla's code of conduct enforcement ladder.

This is an adaptation, not a verbatim adoption. Going clause by clause, there are two substantive differences and one minor one:

Contributor Covenant 2.1The repository original
The Enforcement Responsibilities section (community leaders are obliged to clarify the standards, remove or edit non-compliant content, and explain the reasons for handling it)PresentAbsent — it jumps straight from Scope to Enforcement
A dedicated section on technical disagreementsAbsentPresent — a whole paragraph on "disagreements have to give reasons, give evidence, and make clear what would change your mind"
The list of "behaviours that contribute to a healthy community"5 itemsThe same 5 items, but item 1 adds "assuming good faith", and item 5 newly adds "report problems precisely and specifically"

One more minor difference: in the "Our pledge" section, CC 2.1 lists caste and color; the repository original has neither.

Where the two disagree, the repository original wins.
